Smooth 4-Joint Robotic Arm and Colour Choreographer

The project is a simulated four-joint robotic arm controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller using hardware Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) technology. Four servo motors work together to perform smooth, coordinated dance movements using mathematical cosine ease-in and ease-out interpolation rather than sudden position changes. A common-cathode RGB LED synchronises with the arm's routine, dynamically shifting colours across six distinct choreographic sections. Overall, the system demonstrates advanced PWM control by orchestrating multi-joint mechanical trajectories alongside real-time LED colour modulation.

In-Depth PWM Technology & Signal Architecture

Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) is utilized in two distinct domains in this project: precise pulse-duration timing for mechanical servo positioning, and high-frequency duty-cycle modulation for perceptual color mixing.

⚙

1. Positional Servo PWM (50 Hz)

  • Carrier Frequency: 50 Hz configured via servo.setPeriodHertz(50), providing a standard 20 ms PWM frame interval.
  • Pulse Width Mapping: Attached using servo.attach(pin, 500, 2400), calibrating 500 µs (0°) to 2400 µs (180°) high pulse durations.
  • Timer Pool Allocation: The sketch executes ESP32PWM::allocateTimer(0) through (3) to assign the ESP32's hardware timers to the library's timer pool, allowing the ESP32 PWM peripheral manager to reliably synthesize accurate pulse widths across all four servo lines.
  • Safety Constraints: Target angles are software-constrained to safe limits between MIN_ANGLE = 20° and MAX_ANGLE = 160° (centered at 90°), protecting physical brackets from collisions.
  • Smooth Cosine Easing: Instead of harsh angular steps, movements are partitioned into 100 interpolation steps (MOVE_STEPS = 100) utilizing the ease-in/ease-out formula:
    easeInOut(t) = (1.0 - cos(t * PI)) / 2.0
💡

2. Perceptual LEDC PWM (5 kHz)

  • Carrier Frequency: 5000 Hz (5 kHz) carrier frequency, operating well beyond the human optical flicker threshold to eliminate strobing.
  • Resolution & Duty Cycle: Configured with 8-bit resolution, yielding 256 discrete intensity levels (0 to 255) for precise color calibration.
  • Core 3.x Direct Binding: Initialized using ESP32 Arduino Core 3.x API ledcAttach(pin, 5000, 8) directly binding GPIO pins 25, 26, and 27 without manual channel indexing.
  • Common-Cathode Modulation: Signals driven via ledcWrite(pin, duty). Because the cathode is tied to GND, higher PWM duty cycle delivers higher average forward voltage across each diode anode.
  • Gradual Transition Fading: The fadeToColour() routine interpolates across 50 progressive steps (FADE_STEPS = 50) to generate smooth cinematic fades between performance sections.

Complete Parts List

  • 1 × ESP32 Microcontroller Espressif ESP32 DevKit C V4 with hardware PWM timers and LEDC peripheral controller.
  • 4 × Servo Motors TowerPro SG90 9g positional servo motors operating at 50 Hz PWM (Joints 1–4).
  • 1 × Common-Cathode RGB LED Multi-colour indicator with common cathode wired to GND and independent R, G, B anodes.
  • 3 × Resistors (220 Ω) Current-limiting resistors placed in series with the Red, Green, and Blue anodes.
  • Wokwi Simulator & Virtual Breadboard Simulated circuit canvas with 5V DC power rail and shared ground references.

The Four Servo Motors in the Choreography

Each servo corresponds to an anatomical joint in the robotic chain, working in synergy to execute K-pop-inspired group formations:

Joint 1

Base (GPIO 13)

The horizontal foundation pivot. It rotates the entire arm horizontally across the stage, orchestrating wide directional sweeps and anchoring symmetrical poses.

Joint 2

Shoulder (GPIO 14)

The primary elevation joint. It bears the cantilevered limb load, executing dramatic vertical strokes, deep crouching drops, and tall unison reaches.

Joint 3

Elbow (GPIO 18)

The mid-limb articulation hinge. It flexes in complementary or opposing phase to the shoulder, producing dynamic zig-zag geometry and wave propagation.

Joint 4

Wrist (GPIO 19)

The agile end-effector. With minimal mechanical inertia, it punctuates rhythm with snappy double-pumps, delicate playful bounces, and sharp final hits.

RGB LED Colour Choreographer Schedule

The common-cathode RGB LED synchronises with every beat of the performance, altering its colour palette to accentuate each movement style:

Section 1: Starting Pose · Calm Blue

Centres all four joints at 90° before rising into an asymmetric ready stance. Accompanied by pure blue illumination (R:0, G:0, B:255).

Section 2: Synchronised Hit · Hot Pink

All four joints strike identical angles in unison followed by a fast double-pump beat under high-energy hot pink (R:255, G:0, B:100).

Section 3: Side-to-Side Sway · Mint Green

Odd-numbered joints (1 & 3) tilt oppositely to even-numbered joints (2 & 4), swapping back and forth to mint green (R:0, G:255, B:100).

Section 4: Sequential Wave / Ripple · Warm Orange

A fluid wave ripples sequentially along the arm (1 → 2 → 3 → 4) and returns (4 → 3 → 2 → 1) against glowing warm orange (R:255, G:150, B:0).

Section 5: Playful Bounce · Bright Yellow

High-tempo criss-cross bounces and fast micro-oscillations deliver playful dance ad-libs under radiant yellow (R:255, G:255, B:0).

Section 6: Final Pose & Curtain · Regal Purple to Black

A slow majestic spread snaps into a sharp ending pose under regal purple (R:200, G:0, B:255), bursts into a bright white flash, and fades to black.
